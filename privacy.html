Depthray privacy policy

Last updated 22 September 2026. This policy covers the Depthray app for Meta Quest and Pico headsets, published by Woodford XR, the trading name of Sean Gilleece, a sole trader.

The short version

Depthray collects no personal data. It has no account of its own, no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising. Everything it stores stays on your headset, and it talks to no server you did not set up yourself, apart from the two cases below where you choose to sign in to a service.

What stays on your headset

Depthray keeps, in its own private storage on the headset: the media servers and network shares you add, with their passwords and access tokens encrypted under the headset's own key store; your settings; and your playback history, so a film can resume where you left it. None of this is sent to Woodford XR or to anyone else. Removing a server in the app deletes its details, and uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

What the app connects to

  • Your own servers. When you add an SMB share, a DLNA server, or an Emby, Jellyfin or Plex server, Depthray connects to that server to list and stream your files. Those connections carry your credentials for that server and nothing else. On a home network many of these servers use plain http; the app tells you when an address you enter is not encrypted.
  • Emby Connect and plex.tv. If you choose to sign in to Emby or Plex with your account, Depthray contacts Emby's or Plex's own sign in service, which is how those services hand a player the list of your servers and the tokens to reach them. Those services have their own privacy policies. Depthray sends them only what their sign in needs.
  • The store you bought it from. On Meta Quest the app checks with the Meta Horizon platform that your account is entitled to the app, and uses the same platform for the one in app purchase that unlocks it after the free trial. For this the app reads your Meta user ID on the headset and uses it for nothing else: it is not shown, stored by Woodford XR or sent anywhere but to Meta. The Pico store's equivalent is used on Pico headsets. The stores' own policies cover the download and the purchase.

No other network use exists. There is no telemetry and no call home.

Hands, eyes and cameras

Hand tracking, where you have allowed it, is used only to aim the pointer and to detect a pinch. Eye tracking, on headsets that have it and where you turn it on, aims the same pointer. The passthrough view is composed by the headset's own system; the app never receives the camera image. None of this is recorded, stored or transmitted.

The log and Report a problem

Depthray can keep a plain text log of what it did, in the headset's Download folder, so you can read it or attach it to a report. The Report a problem row in About gathers that log, the headset model, the app version and a note you type, writes them to a file in the same folder, and hands the file to the mail or share app you pick. Nothing is sent unless you choose to send it, you see what it contains first, and the file stays on your headset. The log can name the servers you added and the files you played.

Children

Depthray is for users aged 13 and over, and is not directed at children.

Requests from authorities

Woodford XR holds no data about Depthray's users, so it cannot produce any. Any request from a public authority is reviewed for its legal basis, refused or challenged if it lacks one, answered with no more than the law requires, and recorded.

Your rights and deleting your data

Because nothing about you is held by Woodford XR, there is nothing to request a copy of and nothing to correct. To delete everything Depthray stored, uninstall the app; the headset removes its private storage with it. The optional log and report files in the Download folder are ordinary files you can delete yourself.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with a new date. It will not start collecting data without saying so here.
